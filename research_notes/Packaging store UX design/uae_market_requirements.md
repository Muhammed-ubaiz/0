# UAE and GCC Market Requirements for an Online Packaging Store (B2C and B2B)

General information only, not legal or tax advice. Position as researched on 9 November 2026. Confirm each rule against the official text or with UAE counsel or a tax agent before relying on it.

**Evidence quality.** The official UAE sites moet.gov.ae, moccae.gov.ae and uaelegislation.gov.ae refused automated fetching, and mof.gov.ae pages did not appear in search. Most regulatory facts below therefore come from law-firm or press summaries, not the instruments themselves. Market statistics come mostly from vendor blogs and conflict with each other. ## What are current UAE shopper and business-buyer preferences for device use, payment methods (cards, Apple Pay, COD, BNPL such as Tabby and Tamara), and delivery expectations?

### Takeaway
Cards and wallets are the core, BNPL is a material consumer option, and COD is declining but still widely offered, with much higher failure cost. Exact shares are unreliable: published COD figures range from about 23% to 32% of orders. For B2B, credit terms are normal (about 47% of B2B sales), so a card-only checkout does not fit business buyers.

### Cited Findings
**Payment mix (all directional; sources conflict)**
- One breakdown gives order shares of cards 40-49%, wallets 15-20%, COD 31.6%, BNPL 8-12%, and value shares of cards 45-50%, wallets 20-25%, COD 7-10%, BNPL 15-18%. The article does not tie every row to a source, so value shares and success rates are effectively its own estimates. [Vendor, April 2026] — [Axiom](https://www.axiomxgroup.com/blog/cash-on-delivery-uae)
- Contradicting figures: COD 25-30% of transactions in 2026, down from 40% in 2022 — [Webmedic](https://webmedic.com/best-payment-gateways-ecommerce-uae) [Vendor, Snippet]; COD 23% — [GlobeNewswire quick-commerce report summary](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/01/23/3224680/28124/en/UAE-Quick-Commerce-Market-Report-2026-2031-Emirati-Millennials-Drive-Quick-Commerce-Growth-with-On-Demand-Lifestyle.html) [Snippet; quick commerce is a different basket].
- Wallets (Apple Pay, e& money, PayIt) about 44% of payments in 2025, COD volume down 37% over four years, BNPL used by about 39% of shoppers (an adoption measure, not an order share) — [INTLBM, July 2026](https://intlbm.com/2026/07/15/the-new-face-of-shopping-in-the-uae/) [Vendor/blog, Snippet].
- 71.2% of UAE stores still offer COD; COD usage down 53% since 2020; 74% of buyers would switch to card if COD were removed (attributed to The National, February 2026) — [Axiom](https://www.axiomxgroup.com/blog/cash-on-delivery-uae) [Vendor].

**COD economics (vendor estimates, no primary source for fees)**
- COD delivery success 70-80% against 94% cards and 96% wallets; settlement 14-30 days; typical refusal/return-to-origin 20-30%; phantom orders 3-8% without verification; fees AED 5-15 handling or 1-3%, AED 10-25 per failed attempt, AED 15-30 per return — [Axiom](https://www.axiomxgroup.com/blog/cash-on-delivery-uae) [Vendor].
- Suggested controls: WhatsApp or IVR verification within 30 minutes, prepaid incentive, zone rules, AED 5-15 surcharge, card-on-delivery terminals or payment links, block repeat refusers, restrict COD above about AED 800 — [Axiom](https://www.axiomxgroup.com/blog/cash-on-delivery-uae) [Vendor].

**BNPL regulation and providers**
- Short-term credit including BNPL has been regulated by the Central Bank of the UAE since December 2023 under the Finance Companies Regulation; only licensed entities, or those partnering with licensed institutions, may provide it — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/uae-central-bank-introduces-regulatory-framework-buy-now-pay-later-providers) [Law firm, Snippet].
- Tamara holds a CBUAE restricted finance licence — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/tamara-gains-uae-central-bank-licence-to-expand-fintech-footprint-1.500314060) [Snippet]. Tabby holds a CBUAE Stored Value Facilities licence — [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/fintech/news/tabby-obtains-stored-value-facilities-licence-in-the-uae) [Snippet].
- Etihad Credit Bureau reports include Tabby and Tamara account data from July 2026 — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/uae-credit-reports-to-include-buy-now-pay-later-data-from-tabby-and-tamara-1.500621675) [Snippet].
- User counts of "10m+" (Tabby) and "8m+" (Tamara) appear in one vendor piece and look inflated for UAE alone (likely regional) — [Webmedic](https://webmedic.com/best-payment-gateways-ecommerce-uae) [Vendor, Snippet].

**Fees for payment methods (legal limit)**
- Under the e-commerce law, sellers may not add digital-payment fees beyond those declared in the contract or terms (Art. 15(3)); breach starts at a written warning, rising to AED 10,000-20,000 and 20-day closure on a fourth offence — [Hadef & Partners](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/digital-commerce-platform-compliance-the-new-penalty-framework-for-uae-platforms/) [Law firm]. Also noted by [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102iqny/uae-federal-law-on-modern-trading-by-technological-means/) [Law firm].

**Business buyers**
- Atradius 2026 UAE survey: credit sales about 47% of B2B transactions; about three in five firms offer terms up to one month, about one in three one to two months; around two in five B2B invoices are paid late; leading cause is customer cash-flow constraints (49%). Fieldwork was late Q2 2026 — [Atradius](https://atradius.de/newsroom/reports/b2b-payment-practices-trends-in-united-arab-emirates-2026) [Snippet; research firm with credit-insurance interest].

**Delivery**
- Press framing is that same-day delivery "becomes the new norm" in UAE retail — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business-technology-review/uae-retail-revolution-same-day-delivery-is-the-new-standard) [headline only].
- Same-day or next-day delivery is estimated to lift COD success by 10-15 points — [Axiom](https://www.axiomxgroup.com/blog/cash-on-delivery-uae) [Vendor].
- Sellers must deliver what they declare on timeframes, specifications and logistics costs (Art. 6(1)) — [Hadef & Partners](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/digital-commerce-platform-compliance-the-new-penalty-framework-for-uae-platforms/) [Law firm].

### Inferences
- **Must:** declare any COD, BNPL or card surcharge in terms and at checkout before payment (Art. 15(3)). Show delivery windows and shipping cost only if they can be met (Art. 6(1)).
- **Should, consumers:** lead with Apple Pay and cards; offer Tabby and/or Tamara on mid-basket orders; keep COD but constrain it by order value, emirate and customer history.
- **Should, business buyers:** offer quote requests, bank transfer against pro-forma, and vetted credit accounts with short terms (up to 30 days matches the most common practice). Given about 40% late payment, gate credit behind approval.
- BNPL is a consumer credit product, so it is unlikely to suit company purchases or bulk orders. Hide it for business accounts unless the provider confirms otherwise.
- Packaging differs from the fashion and grocery baskets behind most of these statistics: bulky cartons and repeat consumables make delivery cost, scheduled delivery, and reorder more important, and same-day claims less achievable for pallet quantities.

### Gaps
- No credible, sourced mobile share found. A figure of 78.67% of orders surfaced in search from a low-authority blog, and I could not attribute it reliably. DataReportal, Visa and Checkout.com primary reports were not retrieved.
- A PwC figure (63% of UAE consumers would pay more for same-day, 54% in Saudi Arabia, possibly 2024) surfaced in search, but I could not confirm the page or edition.
- Blog claims that half of shoppers expect delivery in under two hours, and that next-day is the Dubai baseline, could not be traced to a survey.
- No data on UAE B2B e-commerce buyer behaviour (device, online procurement, bank transfer share), or on packaging buyers specifically.
- Tabby and Tamara merchant eligibility, order limits, and whether they support B2B in the UAE: not confirmed. Tabby's reported SME finance licence is Saudi only.
- Whether Federal Decree-Law 6 of 2025 changed BNPL rules: not found.

## How is WhatsApp or live chat used in UAE commerce, and how should it be integrated?

### Takeaway
WhatsApp is the leading business contact channel in the best available survey, ahead of call centres and email, and respondents strongly want a human on the other end. Treat it as a primary sales and service channel with human handoff, but not as a replacement for the legally required invoice, terms and complaints process.

### Cited Findings
- YouGov for Zbooni (vendor-commissioned), 1,000 UAE residents, published February 2024: 65% used WhatsApp for a product or service inquiry in the past year, against 55% call centre, 48% email, under 30% social inboxes; 85% prefer WhatsApp to interact with companies; 85% want all businesses to offer it for support; 84% find it more helpful than email; 87% want a real person rather than a chatbot or AI — [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/85-percent-of-uae-residents-want-businesses-to-use-whatsapp/).
- WhatsApp or IVR order confirmation within 30 minutes is estimated to cut COD return-to-origin by 8-12 points — [Axiom](https://www.axiomxgroup.com/blog/cash-on-delivery-uae) [Vendor estimate].
- The e-commerce law covers websites, platforms, apps and social media — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102iqny/uae-federal-law-on-modern-trading-by-technological-means/) [Law firm].
- Sellers must provide a complaints-handling mechanism (Art. 6(6)-(8)) and issue a detailed invoice for each purchase (Art. 5(8)) — [Hadef & Partners](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/digital-commerce-platform-compliance-the-new-penalty-framework-for-uae-platforms/) [Law firm].
- The Personal Data Protection law (Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021) sits alongside these rules — [Hadef & Partners](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/have-a-website-application-or-other-digital-platform-in-the-uae-here-s-what-you-need-to-know-about-the-uae-consumer-protection-law/) [Law firm].

### Inferences
- **Must:** sales closed in WhatsApp fall within the e-commerce law's "social media" channel reach, so the same invoice, terms and Arabic obligations apply. Send a compliant invoice and link terms from the chat.
- **Should:** place a WhatsApp entry on product pages, the bulk-quote page, cart, order confirmation and help pages. Pre-fill the message with SKU, quantity and page context.
- **Should:** use WhatsApp Business Platform (API) rather than a single phone app, so staff share an inbox and orders connect to the CRM. Keep a bot only for triage, status and reorders, with visible human handoff. Publish service hours in Gulf time and route by language.
- **Should:** collect opt-in separately for marketing; keep transactional messages (confirmation, dispatch, COD verification) distinct.
- For B2B, WhatsApp fits quotes, artwork approvals and reorders. Mirror the final quote and approval to email or the account, for an audit trail.
- Keep a non-WhatsApp route (email/form) so that complaints handling does not depend on one channel.

### Gaps
- Figures seen in search but not reliably attributable: WhatsApp penetration above 90% (attributed to DataReportal), 7.1m UAE users (Sensor Tower estimate) against 9.2m (no source), and AED 0.212 per marketing message since 1 October 2026 (attributed to Meta). Verify pricing in Meta's rate card.
- The survey is from 2024, commissioned by a messaging vendor, and measures stated preference and inquiries, not purchases.
- UAE telemarketing and direct-marketing rules as applied to WhatsApp were not researched. No survey on B2B WhatsApp use found.

## What are the requirements and best practices for displaying AED prices, 5% VAT, tax invoices and TRN, and any current UAE e-invoicing obligations or consumer-protection and e-commerce rules?

### Takeaway
Consumer-facing prices must include VAT. Invoices and website information must be available in Arabic. E-commerce sellers have had a graduated penalty schedule since 28 November 2025. B2B e-invoicing is the major upcoming change: a store under AED 50m revenue must appoint an Accredited Service Provider by 31 March 2027 and go live on 1 July 2027, while B2C stays out of scope for now.

### Cited Findings
**VAT-inclusive pricing**
- Prices for taxable supplies must be displayed inclusive of VAT (Art. 38 VAT Law, Federal Decree-Law 8 of 2017; Art. 27(1) Executive Regulation, Cabinet Decision 52 of 2017). Supplies to VAT-registered businesses and exports may be priced exclusive of VAT if that is "clearly stated" (Arts. 27 and 70(6)) — [Habib Al Mulla via Legal 500, 8 August 2025](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-arab-emirates/tax/vat-inclusive-displayed-prices-rule-exceptions-and-implications) [Law firm].
- Silence is risky: the same article's example has a VAT-registered supplier bearing AED 50,000 where AED 1,050,000 was agreed with no VAT reference — [same source](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-arab-emirates/tax/vat-inclusive-displayed-prices-rule-exceptions-and-implications).
- Penalty for non-compliant price display: AED 5,000 per violation, reduced from AED 15,000 by Cabinet Decision 49 of 2021 — [same source](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-arab-emirates/tax/vat-inclusive-displayed-prices-rule-exceptions-and-implications). **Possibly superseded:** Cabinet Decision 129 of 2025 revised general tax penalties — [Wafeq](https://www.wafeq.com/en-ae/tax-and-reporting/uae-e-invoicing-penalties) [Vendor]. I could not confirm whether it changed this item.

**Dirham symbol**
- The Central Bank introduced a new dirham symbol in March 2025 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirati_dirham_sign). It is encoded as U+20C3 in Unicode 18.0, released September 2026 — [Unicode blog](https://blog.unicode.org/2026/02/from-central-bank-to-code-point-roadmap.html); [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2026/09/22/uae-dirham-symbol-keyboard-unicode) [Snippets].
- Native rendering depends on each operating system, browser and font; web-font stopgaps exist, such as the `dirham` npm package — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/pooyagolchian/the-uae-dirham-currency-symbol-u20c3-why-it-took-18-years-and-how-to-use-it-today-5f3h) [Snippet].

**Tax invoices (from vendor summaries; verify against the Executive Regulation)**
- A tax invoice carries the words "Tax Invoice", the supplier's name, address and 15-digit TRN, date, description and VAT-inclusive total; a full invoice adds a sequential number, VAT rate and amount per line, and buyer details for B2B — [Wafeq](https://www.wafeq.com/en-ae/tax-and-reporting/vat-invoice-requirements-in-uae); [Flick](https://www.flick.network/en-ae/types-of-vat-invoices-uae) [Vendor, Snippet].
- Simplified invoices are generally available for non-registered customers, or registered customers where the supply is AED 10,000 or less — [Flick](https://www.flick.network/en-ae/types-of-vat-invoices-uae) [Vendor, Snippet].

**E-invoicing (B2B and B2G)**
- Framework: Ministerial Decisions 243 and 244 of 2025 — [Hawksford, updated 14 July 2026](https://www.hawksford.com/insights-and-guides/uae-e-invoicing) [Advisory].

| Phase | Who | ASP appointed by | Mandatory from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot / voluntary | Working group / any business | n/a | 1 July 2026 |
| Phase 1 | Revenue at or above AED 50m | 30 October 2026 (extended from 31 July 2026) | 1 January 2027 |
| Phase 2 | Revenue below AED 50m | 31 March 2027 | 1 July 2027 |
| Government entities | n/a | 31 March 2027 | 1 October 2027 |

  Source for table — [Hawksford](https://www.hawksford.com/insights-and-guides/uae-e-invoicing). **Conflict:** [Wafeq](https://www.wafeq.com/en-ae/tax-and-reporting/uae-e-invoicing-penalties) still shows the earlier 31 July date, and Hawksford does not name the amending instrument.
- B2C invoices are "currently out of scope of the mandate until a later phase is announced" — [Hawksford](https://www.hawksford.com/insights-and-guides/uae-e-invoicing). Scope includes B2B, B2G, intercompany and free-zone transactions with UAE tax obligations; PINT AE requires 51 data fields on tax invoices — [Wafeq](https://www.wafeq.com/en-ae/tax-and-reporting/uae-e-invoicing-penalties) [Vendor].
- Penalties (Cabinet Decision 106 of 2025): AED 5,000 per month for late system implementation or ASP appointment; AED 100 per missed e-invoice or credit note, capped at AED 5,000 per month; AED 1,000 per day for late notice of system failure or registration changes — [Hawksford](https://www.hawksford.com/insights-and-guides/uae-e-invoicing).
- Guidelines version 1.1 was published June 2026 — [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/06/09/uae-publishes-updated-electronic-invoicing-guidelines-version-1-1-june-2026/) [Snippet]. A version 2.0 was reportedly issued in late October 2026 — [VATCalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/uae/uae-e-invoice-plans-2022/) [Snippet, unconfirmed].

**Consumer protection (Federal Law 15 of 2020, amended by Federal Decree-Law 5 of 2023; Executive Regulations, Cabinet Decision 66 of 2023, effective 14 October 2023)**
- All data, advertising, contracts, website information and invoices must be in Arabic; other languages may be added — [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/uae-consumer-protection-regime-revamped) [Law firm].
- E-commerce suppliers must provide a detailed statement of each product's nature, specifications, handling terms and risks in the original language and Arabic; platforms are responsible for third-party sellers' failures — [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/uae-consumer-protection-regime-revamped) [Law firm].
- Online traders must describe goods accurately, display prices clearly, avoid misleading ads, and inform consumers of warranty, return and refund rights — [Hadef & Partners](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/have-a-website-application-or-other-digital-platform-in-the-uae-here-s-what-you-need-to-know-about-the-uae-consumer-protection-law/) [Law firm].
- "Harmful terms" are prohibited, including "goods sold are neither returnable nor substitutable" (save specific circumstances) and unilateral amendment rights; this prohibition applies to B2B and B2C — [Hadef & Partners](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/have-a-website-application-or-other-digital-platform-in-the-uae-here-s-what-you-need-to-know-about-the-uae-consumer-protection-law/) [Law firm].
- Administrative penalties reported: AED 250,000 for a harmful term, incorrect description or defect mishandling; AED 100,000 for missing Arabic invoice, contract or advertising, or for a misleading price announcement; possible doubling on repeat, closure or licence cancellation — [Hadef & Partners](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/have-a-website-application-or-other-digital-platform-in-the-uae-here-s-what-you-need-to-know-about-the-uae-consumer-protection-law/). The maximum under the law is reported as AED 2m and/or two years' imprisonment — [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/uae-consumer-protection-regime-revamped).
- If a discount is offered, the supplier must notify customers within one week of purchase, or they may claim the difference — [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/uae-consumer-protection-regime-revamped) (the summary is compressed; check the regulation text).

**E-commerce law (Federal Decree-Law 14 of 2023, Trading by Modern Technological Means)**
- Covers commercial activity via websites, platforms, apps and social media, and may reach activity from outside the UAE; traders must meet e-security and cyber-safety standards and issue a non-paper invoice — [Reed Smith, 20 October 2023](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102iqny/uae-federal-law-on-modern-trading-by-technological-means/) [Law firm].
- Cabinet Resolution 200 of 2025 sets the penalty schedule, effective 28 November 2025. Obligations include verifying identity and offer/acceptance in a secure environment (Art. 5(3)), detailed invoice per purchase (Art. 5(8)), stated sale terms, specs, timeframes and logistics costs (Art. 6(1)), no obstruction of lawful return or replacement (Art. 7(1)), and a complaints mechanism (Art. 6(6)-(8)). Most first offences draw a 15-day written warning; identity/contract security starts at AED 2,000-10,000. Fines cap at AED 100,000, with closure on repeat — [Hadef & Partners](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/digital-commerce-platform-compliance-the-new-penalty-framework-for-uae-platforms/) [Law firm].

**GCC tax position**
- Standard VAT: Saudi Arabia 15%, Bahrain 10%, Oman 5%; Kuwait and Qatar have not introduced VAT — [PwC Q3 2026](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/2026/gcc-indirect-tax-news-roundup-q3-2026.pdf); [PwC Q2 2026](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/2026/gcc-indirect-tax-news-roundup-q2-2026.pdf) [Snippets].
- Amendments to the Unified GCC VAT Agreement (Saudi approval by Council of Ministers Resolution 887 of 19 May 2026) change intra-GCC supplies, import VAT and data sharing — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/06/middle-east-gcc-vat-framework-update) [Law firm, Snippet]; expected to affect B2C e-commerce — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/gcc-vat-overhaul-set-to-tighten-cross-border-trade-raise-compliance-stakes) [Snippet].

### Inferences
- **Must:** show VAT-inclusive AED prices to the public by default. A trade-price toggle is defensible only behind a verified business account, and each price must be labelled "excl. VAT".
- **Must:** generate Arabic-capable (ideally bilingual) invoices automatically, carrying the store's TRN.
- **Must:** add an Arabic product statement covering specifications, handling and risks. This touches every SKU, so build Arabic fields into the catalogue model.
- **Must:** remove "no returns" wording and unilateral-change clauses from terms, including trade terms. Make returns, warranty, and complaints visible.
- **Should:** capture buyer company name, TRN and (when available) e-invoicing endpoint at B2B signup, and validate the 15-digit format. Choose an ASP-integrated invoicing setup before 31 March 2027 if revenue is below AED 50m.
- **Should:** use an "AED" text label alongside or as a fallback for the new symbol, until font support is confirmed on target devices.
- **Should:** isolate GCC shipping as a separate configuration: VAT differs by country, and Kuwait and Qatar have none.
- Mixed B2C and B2B stores need two invoice paths: B2C remains outside e-invoicing for now, while B2B sales fall in scope from the relevant phase date.

### Gaps
- Official texts could not be opened, so article wording is unverified.
- Whether a business buyer counts as a "consumer" under the wider law: not established. Only the harmful-terms point is confirmed as B2B and B2C.
- A statutory return or cooling-off window, and the exact defect-remedy periods: none found in the summaries read. Whether prices must legally be stated in AED: not confirmed by a source read.
- Effective date of Decree-Law 14 of 2023: search extracts give an enactment date of 4 September 2023, but the page read did not state it.
- One vendor guide said simplified invoices disappear under e-invoicing. Others still describe them as current. Unconfirmed, and it matters for checkout invoicing.
- Claims seen but not verified: AED 2,500 per detected case for non-compliant invoices under Cabinet Decision 129/2025 (from 14 April 2026), and a Cabinet Decision 173 of 2026 on e-invoicing penalties that, if real, could supersede 106/2025.
- Saudi and other GCC e-invoicing and consumer rules were not researched in detail. ZATCA's e-commerce VAT guideline (Issue 2, May 2026) exists but was not read: [ZATCA](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-on-Electronic-Commerce-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf).
- Registration threshold position (AED 375,000 mandatory) was not verified in this research.

## What are the regulatory considerations for food-contact packaging claims and eco or compostable claims in the UAE, including single-use plastic restrictions?

### Takeaway
A federal ban phased in on 1 January 2024 and 1 January 2026 directly affects the catalogue, including bags labelled biodegradable and paper bags thinner than 50 microns. Whether PLA or paper alternatives to the 2026 items are exempt is not confirmed by any official text I could read, so each "ban-compliant" claim needs per-product evidence. Food-contact claims should be supported by ECAS or equivalent documents. "Biodegradable" and "compostable" should not be used loosely.

### Cited Findings
**Federal ban (Ministerial Decision 380 of 2022)**
- Phase 1, from 1 January 2024: import, manufacture and trade of single-use plastic shopping bags banned, "including biodegradable plastic bags" — [Gulf News quoting MOCCAE, 16 December 2025](https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/uae-to-ban-more-single-use-plastic-items-from-january-1-1.500381533) [Press quoting regulator].
- Phase 2, from 1 January 2026: beverage cups and lids; cutlery (spoons, forks, knives, chopsticks); plates; straws; stirrers; Styrofoam food containers and boxes — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/uae-to-ban-more-single-use-plastic-items-from-january-1-1.500381533).
- Single-use bags of any material, including paper, are banned if thinner than 50 microns — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/uae-to-ban-more-single-use-plastic-items-from-january-1-1.500381533).
- Exemptions reported: items clearly labelled for export or re-export and not sold domestically; bags and products made from materials recycled within the UAE; medicine bags, refuse bags, very thin wraps for meat, vegetables and bread; large shopping bags for clothing, electronics and toys — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/uae-to-ban-more-single-use-plastic-items-from-january-1-1.500381533).
- MOCCAE's guide says reusable bags must be washable, free of harmful substances and meet applicable technical standards — [Aletihad](https://en.aletihad.ae/news/uae/4541370/moccae-reveals-details-of-comprehensive-guide-outlining-regu) [Press quoting regulator, Snippet].

**Alternatives (unresolved)**
- Emirates Biotech (a PLA producer) says PLA, paper, wood and recycled-content products are exempt — [Gulf Business](https://gulfbusiness.com/emirates-biotechs-francois-de-bie-on-what-uaes-plastic-ban-means/) [interested party, Snippet].
- A compliance guide states PLA is "widely reported" as accepted but that it could not confirm this from a UAE government source; it says "paper", "plant-based", "biodegradable" or "compostable" wording does not by itself establish compliance, and treats plastic-lined paper as needing product-specific assessment. It treats bagasse as outside the ban — [Ecofy](https://ecofy.io/resources/compliance/uae-single-use-plastic-ban/) [Vendor].

**Emirate-level rules (via Ecofy, which cites government pages)**
- Dubai, Executive Council Resolution 124 of 2023: plastic carrier bags 1 January 2024; all single-use bags 1 June 2024; Styrofoam cups, plates and containers, plus plastic straws, stirrers, table covers and cotton buds 1 January 2025; plastic plates, cutlery and beverage cups and lids 1 January 2026. Reported Dubai fines of AED 200 per violation are unsourced — [Ecofy](https://ecofy.io/resources/compliance/uae-single-use-plastic-ban/) [Vendor].
- Abu Dhabi: specified Styrofoam foodservice products banned from 1 June 2024 — [Ecofy](https://ecofy.io/resources/compliance/uae-single-use-plastic-ban/) [Vendor citing Abu Dhabi Media Office/EAD].

**Food contact**
- MoIAT refers to "Federal Law Decree No. 20 of 2015 on food contact materials" and an ECAS Food Contact Material certificate. For recycled plastic (rPET) in food packaging the facility must document an effective quality system, show passing tests including migration by an accredited UAE laboratory, and provide a risk assessment — [MoIAT, August 2023](https://moiat.gov.ae/en/media-center/news/2023/08/21/rep-workshop) [Regulator]. The page does not say which supply-chain party must hold the certificate, and the instrument name should be checked against the legislation portal.

**Eco claims and marks**
- MoIAT's April 2025 update lists an Oxo-Biodegradable Plastic Products Mark and an Environmental Mark among its conformity marks, with transition periods of 12 months for products in the local market and 6 months for imports — [MoIAT, 9 April 2025](https://moiat.gov.ae/en/media-center/news/2025/04/09/conformity-marks-2025) [Regulator].
- **Conflict:** a vendor guide says Saudi Arabia and the UAE have strictly banned "oxo-degradable" plastics — [Dashan Packing](https://www.dashanpacking.com/gcc-biodegradable-packaging-rules-explained-for-exporters-the-ultimate-2026-guide/) [Vendor, Snippet]. That sits uneasily with the MoIAT mark's continued listing; unresolved.
- Misleading descriptions and advertising are penalised under consumer law (AED 250,000 for incorrect description) — [Hadef & Partners](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/have-a-website-application-or-other-digital-platform-in-the-uae-here-s-what-you-need-to-know-about-the-uae-consumer-protection-law/) [Law firm].

### Inferences
- **Must:** audit the catalogue against the banned lists before listing. Remove or restrict plastic bags, including those labelled biodegradable; bags of any material below 50 microns; and 2026-list plastic items. Selling to a business does not obviously avoid a "trade" ban.
- **Must:** obtain supplier thickness data in microns for paper bags, since paper is usually specified in GSM.
- **Should:** add per-SKU attributes: material, thickness, certifications held, food-contact status, and evidence file. Display "food-safe" only where supplier documents (ECAS certificate, migration test report) support it for that use.
- **Should:** avoid "UAE ban-compliant", "biodegradable" or "eco-friendly" as blanket claims. Prefer specific, evidenced language (for example "certified to EN 13432" only when the certificate is on file) and state composting conditions, since industrial composting access is not established.
- **Should:** take counsel or MOCCAE confirmation on PLA-lined and plastic-lined paper cups, since these are likely to be core SKUs.
- Export-only stock needs clear labelling and must not be sold domestically, so a GCC export catalogue must be separated from UAE stock.

### Gaps
- Official Decision 380 text and the MOCCAE guide were not retrievable, so the definition of "plastic", paper cups' treatment, and federal penalties remain unconfirmed.
- No official UAE rule specific to compostability claims or recognised standards was found. EN 13432 and ASTM D6400 appear only in vendor guidance.
- ECAS procedure, GSO food-contact standards, labelling symbols, and Dubai Municipality product registration requirements for food-contact items: not retrieved.
- Other emirates' rules: not found. GCC single-use plastic rules and SFDA food-contact rules were not covered by the search.

## What are the UX best practices for Arabic and English bilingual, right-to-left storefronts, and how important is Arabic for this audience?

### Takeaway
Arabic is both a legal baseline for consumer-facing information and a conversion issue for Arabic-speaking buyers, but no reliable data shows the size of that effect in the UAE. Build a true RTL experience with separately indexable Arabic pages, while expecting English to carry much of the UAE audience, especially in Dubai and among non-Arab residents.

### Cited Findings
**Importance**
- Legal baseline: data, advertising, contracts, website information and invoices must be available in Arabic; other languages may be added — [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/uae-consumer-protection-regime-revamped) [Law firm].
- A 2024 study of 566 ATM users in Dubai found nearly all non-Arab participants favour English, Arab participants (nationals and expatriates) use Arabic more often, and Dubai residents lean more towards English than those outside it — [Frontiers in Communication](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2024.1355632/full) [Academic; ATM proxy, not e-commerce].
- A study of 231 UAE and 108 Qatar respondents recommended websites be bilingual in English and Arabic — [WARC](https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/news/how_global_should_localise_for_ecommerce/42601) [Snippet; date not confirmed].
- A "70% prefer Arabic" claim is attributed to a 2025 Tamrah Media analysis that could not be located — [Voxire](https://voxire.com/blog/ecommerce-localization-gcc-arabic-2026/) [Vendor, treat as unverified].

**RTL implementation**
- Prices and currencies are generally not expected to display RTL; force LTR on the price element with `direction` and `unicode-bidi` — [Shopline](https://developer.shopline.com/docs/online-store-3-0-themes/best-practice/arabic).
- Icons may need mirror transforms, as changing text direction alone does not produce the expected result — [Shopline](https://developer.shopline.com/docs/online-store-3-0-themes/best-practice/arabic). Mirror arrows and similar navigation controls, but not logos, photos, numerals or media controls — [Website Spec](https://specification.website/spec/i18n/rtl-support/).
- Phone numbers, email and street addresses typically sit inside RTL text but flow LTR — [W3C](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/inline-bidi-markup/).
- Use `<bdi>` to isolate embedded text, preferred over CSS because browsers may ignore CSS — [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/HTML/Element/bdi). WooCommerce uses `<bdi>` for prices — [wp-dreams](https://wp-dreams.com/?p=57662) [Snippet].
- Arabic web fonts that are not subsetted can significantly increase page weight; Cairo, Tajawal and Noto Kufi Arabic are commonly used — [Simplso](https://www.simplso.com/blogs/development/a-complete-guide-to-shopify-rtl-support-for-the-gcc-market); [Saudisoft](https://localization.saudisoft.com/content-localization-for-gcc/) [Vendor, Snippet; exact attribution uncertain].

### Inferences
- **Must:** provide Arabic for the items the law names: product data, terms, advertising, website information and invoices. An English-only store with a partial Arabic layer carries legal risk for consumer sales.
- **Should:** use CSS logical properties; separate Arabic and English URLs with `hreflang`; a text language toggle ("العربية" / "English"), not flags; language chosen separately from delivery country.
- **Should:** test RTL specifically on checkout, address forms, quantity steppers, price-tier tables (MOQ), dimension specs (e.g. 200 x 300 x 100 mm) and invoices, as these are the mixed-direction pages a packaging store relies on. Isolate dimensions, SKUs and prices with `<bdi>`.
- **Should:** use professional Arabic for product specs and legal text, with native-speaker review. Packaging terminology (GSM, microns, kraft, corrugated flute grades) should have a maintained glossary.
- Prioritise for B2C and SME reach: full Arabic on navigation, product content, checkout and legal/invoice content. Lower-priority long-form content can follow later, except where legal requirements apply.
- Ordering: UAE first; a Saudi extension will need stronger Arabic emphasis, and Saudi-specific VAT and rules.

### Gaps
- No credible survey of Arabic versus English preference among UAE online shoppers or business buyers, and no Emirati or expatriate share of e-commerce.
- No evidence on conversion lift from Arabic in the UAE. Vendor claims (e.g. 40-65% conversion loss) are unsourced.
- Eastern versus Western Arabic numeral convention in UAE commerce: no source found.
- Baymard or Material Design guidance on bidirectionality was not retrieved. Dialect guidance (Gulf versus Modern Standard Arabic) appears only in vendor material.
